Status: local review version (not released). A local review version exists on the maintainer's computer. It is not released and has not been approved scientifically. It was built before the 23 November 2026 standard (joint angles as differences of gravity-referenced segment angles, centre of pressure in the foot frame); an update to that standard is being rebuilt. What this is

Treadmill walking from 10 participants at level, 5° and 10° incline, and 5° and 10° decline, plus the transitions between them: 44 recordings. This is a local review version and is not released. The full version has 7,545 strides; the cleaned version has 7,544. Every stride has exactly 150 samples from 0 to 100 percent of the stride.

What we fixed (original vs fixed)

One card per fix. Each card says what was wrong, how much of the data it touched, and what else it could affect. Before-and-after figures show the published data and the fixed data side by side. Example strides are drawn at random, not hand-picked.

Task timing was off in some recordings

What was wrong. The published task intervals (when belt speed or slope changes) were timed on the belt-speed record, not on the motion data. In 4 of 50 treadmill recordings that clock is off by 1.7 to 7 s, and 2 more cannot be checked, so strides near a change can carry the wrong task label.

How we know. We lined up the belt-speed record with the belt speed measured from heel and toe markers. 44 recordings agree within the accepted band.

What we did. We left the six failing recordings out instead of shifting or estimating any interval.

Example. AB09 at 10° incline: the best shift is 6.85 s, and the belt-speed error drops from 0.67 to 0.07 m/s at that shift, so the recording is left out. AB01 at 5° incline is an exact copy of its 10° incline recording and is also left out.

How much was affected

6 of 50 treadmill recordings (12.0%): 1,064 of 8,626 strides the task windows would have produced (12.3%), about 17.4 of 146.3 minutes of walking. Four recordings are off by 1.7 to 7.0 s, two cannot be checked.

Strides are counted as the task windows would have produced them before anything was left out (8,626 in all 50 recordings). Samples do not apply: these strides were never converted.

What else this could affect

  • Checked, not affected Task labels in the 44 recordings we kept. None of them is off by more than half a second. How we know: 44 of 50 recordings agree within 0.5 s (50 samples at 100 per second); the six that do not are left out.
  • Left out Task labels in the six left-out recordings. 294 stride-in or stride-out decisions would change if the clock were corrected, so we released none of them. How we know: 294 changed decisions in the four recordings with a measured shift; the other two cannot be evaluated.
  • Checked, not affected Signal values (angles, moments, powers) in the kept recordings. The clock only decides task labels. Nothing in a kept recording was shifted or edited for it. How we know: The six recordings are removed whole; no interval is shifted or estimated.
  • Left out What each participant still has. AB01 loses 10° decline, 5° decline and 5° incline; AB08 loses 10° incline; AB09 loses 10° incline; AB10 loses level. How we know: Participants lose these conditions from the review version.
  • Not checked Exact position of each speed or slope change in the kept recordings. Only checked to within half a second; finer timing is not checked. No evidence yet.

One recording's pelvis was computed facing backwards

What was wrong. In AB04 level walking the motion-capture software computed the pelvis facing backwards, so its stored tilt has the wrong sign, and so do the thigh and shank angles built on it.

How we know. The published tilt is the mirror image of the tilt computed from the pelvis markers.

What we did. For that recording only, we flipped the sign of the pelvis tilt using the marker evidence and rebuilt the thigh and shank angles from it.

Example. AB04 level walking, right leg: the pelvis tilt is multiplied by +1 there and by −1 in the other 43 recordings.

How much was affected

AB04 level walking only: 165 of 7,562 strides (2.2%), 24,750 of 1,134,300 samples (2.2%). That is 1 of 44 recordings; all 165 strides in it are affected (100%).

Strides are counted in the converted set of 7,562; samples are 150 per stride.

What else this could affect

The pelvis feeds the thigh angle, and the thigh feeds the shank. The hip, knee and ankle angles are measured between neighbouring body segments, so they do not depend on which way the pelvis frame faces.

  • Also wrong, fixed Thigh angle. Rebuilt from the flipped pelvis tilt (thigh = hip + pelvis tilt). How we know: With the flipped tilt the thigh is closer to the marker thigh in 223 of 228 strides checked against markers (114 per leg).
  • Also wrong, fixed Shank angle. Rebuilt from the corrected thigh and the knee. The marker check cannot tell the two signs apart here. How we know: Shank = thigh − knee holds to rounding in all 7,545 released strides; against markers the two signs miss by 2.4° and 2.5° (right leg), because this participant's tilt is small (about 2°).
  • Checked, not affected Pelvis tilt speed. It follows the published tilt, so it follows the fix. How we know: The stored speed matches the slope of the released tilt in all 7,545 strides (lowest agreement 1.00).
  • Checked, not affected Hip angle. Not affected: it is measured relative to the thigh and pelvis segments, not to the lab frame. How we know: It matches the marker hip in 228 of 228 strides checked (114 per leg), and its mean (5.1°) sits between AB04's 5° decline (4.0°) and 5° incline (9.2°) recordings.
  • Checked, not affected Knee and ankle angles. Not affected: both are measured between neighbouring segments. How we know: An independent check against force-plate and marker evidence kept their published sign for this recording.
  • Checked, not affected Hip, knee and ankle moments and powers. Not affected: they are expressed in each body segment's own frame. How we know: The same independent check applied the ordinary moment and power signs here; the ankle moment size (1.91) lies within AB04's other recordings (1.61 to 1.93 N·m/kg).
  • Left out Pelvis side tilt and twist. Also backwards in the source (side tilt flipped, twist turned by 180°); we do not release these. How we know: Side tilt is flipped in 228 of 228 strides checked (114 per leg).
  • Left out Foot direction angle. About 180° off in this recording in the source; we do not release it. How we know: The source value sits near -168° where the markers give about 12°.
  • Checked, not affected Any other recording being backwards. No other treadmill recording is. How we know: Headings of the other 49 recordings lie within ±4.75°, and 11,710 of 11,715 strides carry the published tilt sign; AB04's other four recordings are within 1.0°.
  • Not checked Five strides at the end of a trial, where the person turns around. In 3 recordings the sign vote is uninformative there; a separate stride check has not been done. No evidence yet.

Pelvis tilt direction follows one sign rule

What was wrong. The source reports forward pelvis tilt as positive. LocoHub's rule (counterclockwise is positive) makes forward tilt negative.

How we know. We checked the sign of each angle and moment against marker and force-plate evidence.

What we did. We multiplied pelvis tilt by −1 in 43 of the 44 recordings (all strides). Hip, knee and ankle angles and the hip, knee and ankle moments keep their published sign.

Does the rule flip pelvis tilt, and which way is forward?

Example. AB01 level walking, right leg: pelvis tilt is multiplied by −1; hip, knee and ankle are unchanged.

How much was affected

All 44 recordings, 100%: 7,547 of 7,562 strides (99.8%; the other 15 were too short or long to convert), 1,132,050 of 1,134,300 samples. In 43 recordings the sign is flipped; in AB04 level it is flipped twice (once for the backwards pelvis, once for the rule), so it ends up as published.

Strides are counted in the converted set of 7,562; the 15 too short or long to convert never reached this step.

What else this could affect

Only the pelvis is flipped. The thigh and shank are built from the pelvis, so they follow it. The hip, knee and ankle angles are published signed in the direction of their name and are not touched.

  • Checked, not affected Thigh and shank angles. They are built after the sign step, so they already use the flipped tilt. How we know: Thigh = hip + pelvis tilt and shank = thigh − knee hold to rounding in all 7,545 released strides (largest difference 0 rad).
  • Checked, not affected Pelvis tilt speed. It was flipped along with the angle. How we know: The stored speed matches the slope of the released tilt in all 7,545 strides.
  • Checked, not affected Hip, knee and ankle angles. Not flipped; each already points the way its name says. How we know: Against markers, the published sign matched in 16 of 16 legs checked for each of hip, knee and ankle.
  • Checked, not affected Hip, knee and ankle moments. Not flipped. How we know: Against force plates the published sign matched in 16 of 16 legs for the knee and ankle moments and 14 of 16 for the hip moment (the weakest of the three).
  • Checked, not affected Powers. Their sign is set separately (see the power card). How we know: Power signs come from the moment-times-speed check, not from the pelvis rule.
  • Left out Foot angle. Would follow the same sign rule, but it is not released. How we know: Foot angle carries the unchecked ankle zero point.
  • Not checked Other datasets' pelvis tilt. Not part of this page; each dataset has its own sign step. No evidence yet.

Thigh and shank angles are rebuilt

What was wrong. Earlier rebuild code added pelvis tilt to the hip angle, but the dataset's authors subtract it, so the thigh angle was off by about twice the pelvis tilt. The older thigh and shank columns are not trusted.

How we know. Hip minus pelvis follows the thigh angle computed from the markers within a few degrees; the markers use the bump at the top of the thigh bone, so a small offset is expected.

What we did. We rebuilt thigh and shank from the published pelvis, hip and knee angles (after the sign step) and release them as side-view estimates.

Example. AB08 at 5° incline, right leg: the earlier rebuild was about 38° off in the example stride.

How much was affected

All 44 recordings, 100%: every one of the 7,547 converted strides (99.8% of 7,562; the other 15 were never converted), 1,132,050 samples each for thigh and shank. The earlier rebuild was off by about 2° to 58° on average, depending on how far the participant tilts the pelvis.

The earlier thigh and shank columns were never released. The error is twice the recording's average pelvis tilt.

What else this could affect

Which way the dependence runs: thigh = hip + pelvis tilt, shank = thigh − knee, foot = shank + ankle. Pelvis, hip, knee and ankle are published by the source; thigh, shank and foot are worked out from them. So a wrong pelvis or hip gives a wrong thigh and shank, and a wrong knee gives a wrong shank only. A wrong thigh never changes the hip or knee.

  • Checked, not affected Knee angle (your example). A bad thigh does not change the knee angle. The knee is published by the source, not worked out from the thigh. The dependence runs the other way: the shank is thigh − knee. How we know: Shank = thigh − knee holds to rounding in all 7,545 released strides, so the knee is an input, not an output; the published knee sign matched markers in 16 of 16 legs.
  • Checked, not affected Hip angle. Not affected: it is published by the source. The old build misused it by adding the pelvis instead of subtracting it. How we know: Hip minus pelvis follows the thigh from markers in 10 of 10 legs in the first check and in 10 of 10 legs in an independent repeat on other recordings.
  • Also wrong, fixed Shank angle. Rebuilt from the corrected thigh and the knee. How we know: Shank = thigh − knee to rounding in all 7,545 released strides.
  • Checked, not affected Thigh and shank speeds. They are the slope of the rebuilt angles, so they follow the fix. How we know: Stored speed matches the slope of the released angle in all 7,545 strides (lowest agreement 1.00).
  • Left out Foot angle (shank + ankle). Would also have been wrong; not released. How we know: Foot angle carries the unchecked ankle zero point.
  • Checked, not affected Hip, knee and ankle moments and powers. Not affected: the source calculates them itself; our thigh and shank are not an input. How we know: Moment and power columns are copied from the source (power after a sign step), not rebuilt from our segment angles.
  • Left out Thigh and shank on the other leg. The earlier tables carried the other leg too; each stride now carries its own leg only. How we know: Earlier opposite-leg columns pre-date the sign step and are not trusted.
  • Not checked How far side-view thigh and shank are from the true 3D angles. They are side-view estimates; the difference to 3D angles is not checked. No evidence yet.

Part of one ankle recording was turned 180°

What was wrong. For 5,429 frames of one recording's right ankle (AB07, 10° incline) the software reported the ankle in an equivalent but different form that shifts it by about 180°. The strides inside sat near −160°.

How we know. After the turn, the strides fall on the strides outside the stretch: −16.2° to 26.8° against −16.6° to 27.0°, and all 20 pass the same angle limits.

What we did. We turned 20 strides back by 180° and logged every one of their 3,000 samples. 2 strides with one-sample jumps are left out. The source copy is untouched.

Example. One stride's tenth sample: −159.6° as published becomes 20.4°. Of the 31 strides in the stretch, 20 were turned back, 2 were left out, and 9 were never part of the converted strides, so they were never released.

How much was affected

AB07, 10° incline, right ankle only: 22 of 7,562 strides (0.29%), 3,300 of 1,134,300 samples; that is 22 of the 82 right-leg strides of that recording (26.8%) and 22 of its 169 strides (13.0%). 20 were turned back (3,000 samples logged), 2 were left out.

Strides are counted in the converted set of 7,562. The source stretch holds 31 strides, but 9 of them were never converted and are not counted.

Only the right ankle angle was in the other form. Thigh and shank do not use the ankle (shank = thigh − knee), so the dependence would only reach the foot angle (shank + ankle), which is not released.

  • Left out Ankle speed. The turn would have broken it, but ankle speed is not a released column. How we know: The released speeds are pelvis, thigh and shank only.
  • Checked, not affected Ankle power in the turned-back strides. It is consistent with the turned-back angle.   • Checked, not affected Ankle moment in the turned-back strides. Not affected. How we know: Moment range 1.46 against 1.74 N·m/kg in the other right-leg strides; no jumps.
  • Checked, not affected Hip and knee angles in the same stretch. Not affected: the 180° form appears only in the ankle. How we know: The 180° form was found in no hip or knee channel; the largest sample-to-sample step in the released right-leg strides is 3.6° (knee) against 4.1° elsewhere.
  • Not checked Sudden knee jumps seen at the edges of the stretch. The source scan saw knee jumps over 90° in 3 strides at these edges; whether they are the 2 strides left out or the 9 never converted was not matched. No evidence yet.
  • Checked, not affected Thigh and shank angles. Not affected: they do not use the ankle. How we know: Shank = thigh − knee holds to rounding in all 7,545 released strides.
  • Left out Foot angle (shank + ankle). Would be 180° off here; not released. How we know: Foot angle carries the unchecked ankle zero point.
  • Checked, not affected The same problem in other recordings. It happens once. How we know: All 500 joint-angle channels were scanned: only AB07/i10 right ankle has a long stretch (5,429 frames).
  • Not checked Two short ankle glitches elsewhere. Left as published, in AB05, 10° decline left ankle (5 frames) and AB06, 5° decline right ankle (8 frames); whether they fall inside released strides was not checked here. No evidence yet.

Power sign was unsettled

What was wrong. Power is stored in two ways (8 recordings differ), and its sign could not be settled from the source alone.

How we know. We kept the sign for which power equals the published moment times the joint speed, with agreement of at least 0.9 on both legs.

What we did. We set the sign that way, and left the power empty where the agreement stays below 0.9.

Does the set sign make power equal moment times joint speed?

Example. AB02 at 10° decline: hip power is multiplied by −1, with agreement −1.00. AB03 at 10° incline: hip power agreement is only 0.80 on one leg and 0.67 on the other, so hip power is left empty there.

How much was affected

12 of 132 power channels (9.1%; 44 recordings × hip, knee, ankle) were not set by a clear rule: 8 left empty and 4 copied. In stride terms: 1,407 of 22,635 stride-joint values left empty (6.2%, 211,050 samples) and 669 copied (3.0%). 16,633 stride-joint values (73.5%, 2,494,950 samples, 96 channels) use a sign set by the moment-times-speed check.

A stride-joint value is one stride's hip, knee or ankle power (7,545 released strides × 3 joints = 22,635). Of the 124 channels with a sign set, 107 were multiplied by −1 and 17 kept as published. Power is stored in two ways; the 8 recordings stored the second way keep their sign: AB03, 10° incline; AB04, 10° decline; AB04, 5° decline; AB04, 5° incline; AB05, 10° decline; AB05, 5° decline; AB05, level; AB05, 10° incline.

What else this could affect

Power relates to the joint moment times the joint speed. We use the published moment and the speed from the published angle to set the power sign; the moment and angle are inputs, the power is the output. Nothing else is computed from power.

  • Checked, not affected Hip, knee and ankle moments. Not changed by the power sign. They are the reference the power sign is checked against. How we know: Their own sign matched force plates in 16 of 16 legs for knee and ankle and 14 of 16 for hip.
  • Checked, not affected Joint angles and angle speeds. Not changed by the power sign. How we know: Stored speeds match the slope of the released angles in all 7,545 strides.
  • Left out Hip power in 6 recordings, plus knee and ankle power in AB03 10° incline. Left empty (present but missing) because the check stayed below 0.9 on at least one leg. How we know: 1,407 stride-joint values; agreement below 0.9 on at least one leg.
  • Not checked The moment or speed in those held recordings. Low agreement could come from the power, the moment or the joint speed. Which one is off was not checked, and moments and speeds have no range check. No evidence yet.
  • Not checked Joint work or energy computed from power by a user. Not computed or checked here. No evidence yet.

What we checked and what we did not

Checked

  • Hip, knee and ankle angles against clearly impossible ranges (the ankle only for impossible values).
  • Every angle (pelvis, hip, knee, ankle, thigh, shank) for sudden jumps between neighbouring samples.
  • That every stride has 150 samples and that values were not changed except where this page says so.

Not checked

  • Moments, powers and all angle speeds: no check was run.
  • Belt speeds.
  • Where the ankle angle is zero.
  • Plausible ranges for each task: we used only coarse limits, not reviewed ranges.
  • Thigh and shank are side-view estimates, not full 3D angles.

A stride that passes has not been shown to be right, only not clearly impossible. Missing samples are never counted as a pass: 376 strides have some missing samples.
