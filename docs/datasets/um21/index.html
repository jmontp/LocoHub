What this is

Treadmill walking from 10 participants at level, 5° and 10° incline, and 5° and 10° decline, plus the transitions between them (belt speed or slope changing): 44 recordings. This is a local review version and is not released. The full version has 7,545 strides; the cleaned version has 7,523. Every stride has exactly 150 samples from 0 to 100 percent of the stride. Joint angles are measured from markers against gravity, power is moment times joint speed, and the pressure point in the foot's frame is published for 60 of 88 legs.

What we fixed and what we did not (original vs fixed)

One card per problem. Each card says what was wrong, how much of the data it touched, what we did, how it was checked, and what else it could affect. Example strides were drawn at random with a recorded seed, not hand-picked.

Task timing

Task timing was off in some recordings

Left out, checked

What was wrong. The published task intervals (when belt speed or slope changes) were timed on the belt-speed record, not on the motion data. In 4 of 50 treadmill recordings that clock is off by 1.7 to 7 s, and 2 more cannot be checked, so strides near a change can carry the wrong task label.

How we know. We lined up the belt-speed record with the belt speed measured from heel and toe markers. 44 recordings agree within half a second.

What we did. We left the six failing recordings out whole instead of shifting or estimating any interval. This is unchanged in this version.

How it was checked. 44 of 50 recordings agree within half a second; the package holds only those 44.

Example from the source. AB09 at 10° incline: the best shift is 6.85 s, and the belt-speed error drops from 0.67 to 0.07 m/s at that shift, so the recording is left out. AB01 at 5° incline is an exact copy of its 10° incline recording and is also left out.

How much of the source this touched, before the fix

6 of 50 treadmill recordings (12.0%): 1,064 of 8,626 strides the task windows would have produced (12.3%), about 17.4 of 146.3 minutes of walking. Four recordings are off by 1.7 to 7.0 s, two cannot be checked.

Strides are counted as the task windows would have produced them before anything was left out (8,626 in all 50 recordings). Samples do not apply: these strides were never converted.

In this version. In this version none of the six is present.

Pelvis, segment and joint angles

Joint angles are measured from markers against gravity

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. The source's own joint angles carry the zero position of the motion-capture software, and that zero is not published. In three recordings probed before the rebuild, the median difference between the published ankle and the ankle from markers was 0.3 to 6.5° and changed with the leg and the trial, so it is not one constant that could be subtracted.

How we know. Thigh, shank and foot angles come from the markers, each in that leg's own side-view plane and measured from gravity (straight down for thigh and shank, forward horizontal for the foot). Hip, knee and ankle angles are differences of these: hip is thigh minus pelvis, knee is thigh minus shank, ankle is foot minus shank. A second implementation recomputed 16 legs and 176 leg-columns of angles and speeds: the largest difference was 0.0 rad and the pattern of missing values was identical.

What we did. All hip, knee, ankle, thigh, shank and foot angles are computed from markers, as differences of segment angles measured from gravity. Foot angle and its speed are released. Missing markers leave the angle missing; nothing is filled.

How it was checked. A second implementation recomputed 16 legs: no difference.

Example from the source. AB06 at 5° incline: the published ankle sat 4.7° (right leg) and 6.1° (left leg) from the ankle from markers at the median.

How much of the source this touched, before the fix

All 44 recordings, 7,545 strides in the full version: every angle column is computed from markers.

The knee marker names are reversed in the source

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. In this source the marker named KNEM lies on the outer (lateral) side of the knee and KNE on the inner (medial) side. That is the reverse of the usual reading of the names. Each leg's side-view plane is built from the knee axis, so the assignment matters.

How we know. For every leg we measured how far each marker sits outward from the middle of the pelvis, along the line between the two front hip markers. In 14 legs of 7 recordings, checked independently, KNEM sat 0.117 to 0.141 m further out than KNE and was nearer the thigh markers in every leg. Across all 88 legs the build found the outer marker at least 0.110 m beyond the inner one. The ankle markers are named the usual way, 0.072 to 0.087 m apart.

What we did. The side-view plane is built with the knee marker KNEM as lateral and KNE as medial.

How it was checked. 14 legs in 7 recordings were checked independently by marker distances: KNEM is lateral in 14 of 14. All 88 legs agree in the build.

Example from the source. Across the 14 legs checked independently, the typical outward distance was 0.13 to 0.20 m for KNEM and 0.006 to 0.061 m for KNE.

How much of the source this touched, before the fix

All 44 recordings, 88 legs, 7,545 strides in the full version.

Legs are counted in the 44 kept recordings (88 legs). The independent check covered 14 of them.

In this version. The reversal is written in the package's list of limitations.

One recording's pelvis was computed facing backwards

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. In AB04 level walking the motion-capture software computed the pelvis facing backwards, so its stored tilt has the wrong sign.

How we know. The published tilt is the mirror image of the tilt computed from the pelvis markers.

What we did. For that recording only, the sign of the pelvis tilt was flipped using the marker evidence. This version keeps that pelvis tilt and compares it with the pelvis from markers in every stride.

How it was checked. The published tilt is the mirror of the marker tilt; the flipped tilt overlaps it.

Example from the source. AB04 level walking, right leg: the pelvis tilt is multiplied by +1 there and by −1 in the other 43 recordings.

How much of the source this touched, before the fix

AB04 level walking only: 165 of 7,562 strides (2.2%), 24,750 of 1,134,300 samples (2.2%). That is 1 of 44 recordings; all 165 strides in it are affected (100%).

Strides are counted in the converted set of 7,562; samples are 150 per stride.

Pelvis tilt direction follows one sign rule

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. The source reports forward pelvis tilt as positive. LocoHub's rule (counterclockwise is positive) makes forward tilt negative.

How we know. We checked the sign of each angle and moment against marker and force-plate evidence.

What we did. Pelvis tilt is multiplied by −1 in 43 of the 44 recordings (all strides); in AB04 level it is flipped twice, so it ends as published. Hip, knee and ankle are differences of marker angles and keep the direction of their names.

How it was checked. Signs were checked against marker and force-plate evidence in 16 legs.

Example from the source. AB01 level walking, right leg: pelvis tilt is multiplied by −1; hip, knee and ankle are not changed by the rule.

Thigh and shank angles are now measured, not built from a formula

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. Earlier rebuild code added pelvis tilt to the hip angle, but the dataset's authors subtract it, so the thigh angle was off by about twice the pelvis tilt. The older thigh and shank columns are not trusted.

How we know. Hip minus pelvis followed the thigh angle computed from the markers within a few degrees; the markers use the bump at the top of the thigh bone, so a small offset was expected.

What we did. Thigh and shank are no longer built from the published pelvis, hip and knee. They are measured from markers in each leg's own plane and released with a foot angle.

How it was checked. A second implementation recomputed 16 legs: largest difference 0.0 rad.

Example from the source. AB08 at 5° incline, right leg: the earlier rebuild was about 38° off in the example stride.

How much of the source this touched, before the fix

All 44 recordings, 100%: every one of the 7,547 converted strides (99.8% of 7,562; the other 15 were never converted), 1,132,050 samples each for thigh and shank. The earlier rebuild was off by about 2° to 58° on average, depending on how far the participant tilts the pelvis.

The earlier thigh and shank columns were never released. The error is twice the recording's average pelvis tilt.

In this version. The earlier formula and its columns are gone.

Part of one ankle recording was turned 180°

No longer needed

What was wrong. For 5,429 frames of one recording's right ankle (AB07, 10° incline) the software reported the ankle in an equivalent but different form that shifts it by about 180°. The strides inside sat near −160°.

How we know. After the turn, the strides fell on the strides outside the stretch: −16.2° to 26.8° against −16.6° to 27.0°.

Joint power

Joint power is recomputed as moment times joint speed

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. The source's powers do not equal the published moment times the joint speed, and their sign could not be settled from the source alone. Against the moment times our new joint speed, the source's power differed per stride by a median of 12% (hip), 9% (knee) and 15% (ankle) of the size of the new power; for the larger gaps (90th percentile) 27%, 18% and 24%.

How we know. Per stride, the gap is the square root of the summed squared difference between the source's power and the new power, divided by the square root of the summed squared new power, over the samples where both exist. The median is over strides.

What we did. Hip, knee and ankle power are now the published moment times our own joint speed: hip is the thigh speed minus the pelvis speed, knee is the thigh speed minus the shank speed (flexion positive), and ankle is the foot speed minus the shank speed. The source's power values are not carried over. Power is missing wherever the moment or a speed is missing. The 1,407 power entries once held back for an unsettled sign are gone, and 174 entries now record power as missing. After the change the gap is zero.

How it was checked. A separate readback of every row found power equal to moment times speed to 1e-12 and 29 other columns identical to the earlier version. An independent check of 10 strides drawn with a recorded seed agreed to better than 1e-9 on every row.

Example from the source. Ankle: the median stride differed by 15% before, the largest of the three joints; its zero and definition changed in this version.

Pressure point

Pressure point on an estimated plate position, published leg by leg

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. The source records the pressure point in each force plate's own frame and does not record where the plate sits in the laboratory. A pressure point in the foot's own frame therefore needs an estimated plate position, and that estimate has to be tested before anyone uses it.

How we know. The pressure point is expressed in the ankle frame, which turns with the foot, inside that leg's side-view plane; its origin is 100 mm forward of the ankle joint centre along the foot, and sideways is out of the plane. The plate's axis and sign come from the ankle path, its tilt from the belt slope, its forward shift from the hip and knee moments, its height from the rule that the foot rests on the belt, and its sideways shift from the mean foot position.

What we did. A leg publishes the pressure point only if three checks pass: the ankle moment, held out of the fit, is reproduced (correlation at least 0.95); the toe-off end of the pressure point lies within 4 cm of the toe marker in at least 95% of strides (at 0.2 body weight); and the plate is loaded at the leg's heel strikes in at least 95% of them. Each leg is judged on its own, so a passing leg publishes even when the other leg of the recording is held. 60 of 88 legs pass, and 5,141 strides carry a value. The other 28 legs (2,383 strides) have the three columns present but empty, each with its reason. Published values are labelled 'estimated plate position'.

How it was checked. A second implementation recomputed 16 legs: the unheld pressure point agreed to 7e-16 m and the verdicts were identical. The pass or hold decision of all 88 legs was recomputed from the per-leg numbers: 60 pass, 28 held. Not independent: the plate axis, sign, tilt and shifts were taken from the build's summary.

Example from the source. AB01 level: both legs publish (held-out ankle moment correlation 0.998, toe-off within 4 cm in 100% of strides). AB07 at 10° decline: the right leg is held (toe-off within 4 cm in 90% of strides) and the left leg publishes alone.

How much of the source this touched, before the fix

28 of 88 legs (31.8%) are held: 2,383 of 7,545 strides (31.6%) have no pressure point. 60 legs publish one for 5,141 strides.

Legs are counted in the 44 kept recordings (88 legs). Of the 5,162 strides in the published legs, 21 have no loaded plate samples and carry no value, so 5,141 carry one.

In this version. 60 of 88 legs publish the pressure point and 28 are held, each with a reason.

Held-back data: five recordings left as they are

What we checked and what we did not

Checked

  • The cleaned version is the full version minus 22 failures and nothing else: every kept stride has identical values in both, every stride has exactly 150 samples from 0 to 100 percent, and every left-out stride has a row and a reason (39 rows). A second agent recomputed this from the files.
  • Hip, knee and ankle angles against coarse physical limits, all angles for sudden jumps between neighbouring samples, and the pressure point against its distance from the foot. 7,523 of 7,545 strides pass; 22 fail.
  • Angles, speeds and the pressure point were recomputed by a second implementation on 16 legs: angles and speeds agree to 0.0 rad and the pressure point to 7e-16 m.
  • Power equals moment times joint speed to 1e-12 in a separate readback of every row, and to 1e-9 in an independent check of 10 strides.
  • The pressure-point decision (published or held) was recomputed for all 88 legs: 60 published, 28 held, identical to the build.
  • No angle jump of 30° or more between neighbouring frames in 1,031 stride windows of 12 legs, by a simpler second method.

Not checked

  • Joint speeds and joint moments have no limit, and power has none either (it is exact moment times speed but not checked for size).
  • Pelvis, thigh, shank and foot angles were checked for jumps only, not for range.
  • Belt speeds, and plausible ranges for each task: only coarse limits written before any of these data were read were used. They are not approved reference ranges.
  • The plate position is fitted, not measured: the pressure point was not compared with a measured foot-frame value, and its axis, sign, tilt and shifts were taken from the build's summary in the independent check.
  • The knee marker names rest on marker positions only; no photograph or marker-set description was seen.
  • The two jump strides were judged on the source ankle and not judged again on the ankle from markers.
  • The cause of the toe-off failure in the held-back 10° incline recordings.

A stride that passes has not been shown to be right, only not clearly impossible. A passing check is not scientific approval. Missing samples are never counted as a pass.
