What this is

Movement recorded from 22 participants during level-ground walking, ramps, stairs, the transitions between them, and treadmill walking: 3,146 recordings. This is a local review version and is not released. The full version has 84,793 observations; the cleaned version has 83,547. An observation is one stride of one leg (81,647 in the full version, 150 samples from 0 to 100 percent of the stride) or one whole recording (3,146; every recording is exported once, with its source sample times at 200 Hz). Each file comes as a phase table and a time table.

What we fixed and what we did not (original vs fixed)

One card per problem. Each card says what was wrong, how much it touches, what we did, how it was checked, and what else it could affect. Modes are level ground (680 recordings), ramps (1,394), stairs (916) and treadmill (156). Cards that show an example say how it was drawn; the figures that show every participant have no example to pick.

Timing of events on the treadmill

Left treadmill toe-off was a copy of the right foot's

Fixed and checked

What was wrong. In all 157 treadmill recordings of the source, the left toe-off column is identical to the right one, while the heel strikes differ. The shared toe-off falls where the right belt unloads, so the left stance ran from the left heel strike to the right foot's next toe-off. That is about 0.08 to 0.14 of the stride (median per participant), against 0.58 to 0.63 for the right foot.

How we know. A read-only scan of all 3,147 source recordings found the copy in 157 of 157 treadmill recordings and in none of 2,990 others. In one recording per participant the left belt stays loaded about 0.4 to 0.5 s after the shared toe-off, while the right belt unloads at it.

What we did. We rebuilt the left toe-off as the first sample after each left heel strike, inside the stride, where the left belt vertical force is below 20 N (the contact rule already used elsewhere). 20,431 of the 20,433 left treadmill steps got a toe-off; 2 have none, because the belt force never falls below 20 N, and their stance fields stay empty. No threshold was tuned. The right foot and every other mode are untouched. Only the left stance fields changed; no signal value changed. 18,667 left-foot treadmill strides are in the package; 1,778 left treadmill steps are left out by the authors' rule and listed.

How it was checked. A second agent re-scanned the source (157 of 157, 0 of 2,990), recomputed 60 random left toe-offs from the exported force with no mismatch, and compared old and new exports: the same 84,793 observations in the same order, the same left-out list, and no angle, speed, moment, power, force or pressure column changed.

In this version. The median left stance is now 0.587 to 0.633 of the stride per participant, next to 0.579 to 0.631 for the right foot.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22
  • Kinds of recordings. Treadmill only: the copy is in 157 of 157 treadmill source recordings and in none of the 2,990 others
  • Share of the data. 18,667 of 84,793 observations (22.0%) are left-foot treadmill strides

Example. The first figure shows one recording per participant, all 22, so nothing was picked. Right-foot dots sit on the line; left-foot dots sit far below it.

What else this could affect

  • Also wrong, fixed Left stance and swing timing of treadmill strides. Stance now ends at the left belt's unloading. How we know: Median left stance per participant 0.587 to 0.633 of the stride, was 0.083 to 0.140.
  • Checked, not affected Angles, speeds, moments, powers, forces and pressure point. No data column changed between the old and new export; only the stance fields in the observation notes changed. How we know: Streamed old-versus-new compare for all 22 participants by the worker, re-done for 3 by a second agent: 0 unexpected differences.
  • Checked, not affected Right foot and all other modes. The rule is applied to the left foot of treadmill recordings only. How we know: 86,795 steps that are not left treadmill steps are identical before and after.
  • Left out Stance of 2 left strides. Their stance fields are empty because no sample falls below 20 N inside the stride. Nothing was invented. How we know: AB12 1, AB21 1.
  • Open limit Left toe-off timing. It is a rule, not a measurement. It is tested on the right foot only; the left belt is assumed to behave the same way. How we know: Right-foot test: median 0 ms, 95th percentile 15 ms, worst 195 ms over 20,447 strides.
  • Not checked Left toe-off against an independent event. No video, marker or second plate event was used to confirm the rebuilt left toe-off.

Early source heel strikes give very short left stances

Kept and flagged, nothing changed

What was wrong. In 20 left treadmill strides the source heel strike falls while the left foot is still coming off the previous step, so applying the 20 N rule gives a stance of only 2 to 5 samples. In every case the force at the heel strike is above 20 N (100 to 590 N) and drops below 20 N within a few samples, while the stride has 40 to 73 samples below 20 N.

How we know. A second agent examined three of the strides: the left force is still falling off the previous contact, or is near zero, when the heel strike is marked, and real loading starts in the last one or two samples of the stride. That points to an early source heel-strike time, not to a toe-off error.

What we did. Nothing was tuned or moved. Of the 20 strides, 2 are left out by the authors' rule (they have more than two belt speeds) and 18 stay in the package, flagged. 12 of the 18 fail a coarse limit and are left out of the cleaned version; 6 remain in it. The heel-strike timing is a source question that is not resolved.

How it was checked. The audit list of 22 short stances was accounted one by one by a second agent: 18 flagged early heel strikes, 2 flagged as having no toe-off, 2 left out by the authors' rule, 0 unaccounted.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 4 of 22 (AB12 12 strides, AB27 3, AB21 2, AB30 1)
  • Kinds of recordings. Left foot, treadmill only, 6 recordings
  • Share of the data. 18 of 84,793 observations (0.02%)

Example. The figure shows 8 of the 18 strides drawn at random with seed 20261201. Each line is the left belt force at the heel strike and the six samples after it.

What else this could affect

  • Open limit Stance and swing timing of these 18 strides. Stance is 2 to 5 samples long and is not a real stance. How we know: 18 observations in 6 recordings; flagged in the data.
  • Open limit Which strides fail a coarse limit. 12 of the 18 fail a limit (against 1.5% of all observations). The cause was not examined. How we know: 6 of the 18 remain in the cleaned version.
  • Checked, not affected Signal values inside the stride. Nothing was altered; the strides are exactly as the rule cut them.
  • Not checked Short stances on the right foot, in other modes, or with a stance above 5 samples. The audit lists left treadmill strides only.

Force plates and missing values

Some force samples look like a plate that dropped out

Kept and flagged, nothing changed

What was wrong. In these samples the vertical force reads 0 while the ankle moment is above 0.3 N·m per kg. The two disagree, which looks like a force plate that dropped out.

How we know. A second agent recomputed the flag from the exported tables for two participants (AB06 and AB18) and every 200 Hz recording of both: 0 mismatches.

What we did. The samples are flagged only. They are not removed, the observation is not failed for it, and nothing is filled in. 628 of the 689 observations remain in the cleaned version; the other 61 are left out for other reasons. Treating dropout as failing (689 more observations out of the cleaned version) or blanking the moments is possible and was not done.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes; 203 recordings (503 strides and 186 whole recordings)
  • Share of the data. 689 of 84,793 observations (0.8%); 33,465 flagged samples

Example. The figure counts flagged samples for all 22 participants; nothing was drawn. AB21 (5,296), AB12 (5,262), AB30 (4,943) and AB07 (3,216) have the most samples.

What else this could affect

  • Open limit Ankle moment and power in flagged samples. Force and moment disagree there, and which one is right was not examined. Both are kept as published. How we know: 33,465 samples in 689 observations.
  • Checked, not affected Joint angles and pressure point. They come from the markers or are separate columns and are not touched by the flag.
  • Not checked Other plate problems. Only the pattern force 0 with a large ankle moment is flagged; other kinds of plate errors are not searched for.

Stance force is masked where no plate carries the foot

Held back, left as it is

What was wrong. During stance, where no plate carries at least 20 N under the foot, the force cannot be assigned to that foot. This is a measurement limit of the source.

How we know. The 20 N threshold was fixed before results, as 1.5 times the roughly 13 N offset of the level-ground treadmill plate (the same rule as for Georgia Tech 2023). A separate readback found 0 masked stance samples at or above 20 N and 0 unmasked stance samples below 20 N.

What we did. Vertical, forward and sideways force are set to missing in those samples, and so are the moments, power and pressure point that depend on them; nothing is set to zero. A stride is flagged when at least half of its stance samples have no force.

How it was checked. The masks for all 3,146 recordings equal an independent recomputation, and a scalar loop over 66 random recordings has 0 mismatches.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22
  • Kinds of recordings. Level ground 10,766 strides (75.5% of its strides), ramps 12,533 (62.8%), stairs 3,961 (39.4%), treadmill 0 after the left toe-off fix
  • Share of the data. 27,260 of 84,793 observations (32.1%); 33.4% of the 81,647 strides

Example. The figure counts all strides by mode; nothing was drawn.

What else this could affect

  • Left out Force, moments, power and pressure point in masked stance samples. Empty, not zero. Averages over strides must skip them. How we know: 27,260 flagged strides; 242 of them also fail a coarse limit.
  • Open limit Left-right side check. It could not run in 40,800 strides because force is masked in stance, mostly on ramps and transitions. Missing is not a pass.
  • Open limit Forward and sideways force. At the first export, 475,592 phase force samples were missing without being masked: forward and sideways force is missing where a loaded foot fails a 45 degree heading gate, or where no plate is loaded. The cause was not diagnosed per case. How we know: Second implementation agrees with the count.
  • Checked, not affected Joint angles. Angles come from markers, not plates.

Held frames: angles are missing where the model fit was bad

Held back, left as it is

What was wrong. In a held frame the fit of the body model to the markers was bad, or a marker was missing, so the angles of that leg cannot be trusted. Overall 1,132,257 of 28,831,366 leg-frames (3.9%) are held.

How we know. A frame of one leg is held when a pelvis, thigh or shank marker fit leaves a residual over 20 mm, or when the knee axis is more than 45 degrees from the lateral knee marker. The two thresholds were fixed from the pilot distributions (medians 1 to 5 mm and 4 to 10 degrees), not from pass rates.

What we did. Nothing is filled. In a held frame the angles, angle speeds and pressure point of that leg are missing, and so is power, which needs the speed; forces and moments are kept. The share of held frames is written in each observation's notes.

How it was checked. The readback found every held column empty and no value inside a held frame. Pelvis angles were re-derived from the raw markers with scalar loops for 66 random recordings (largest difference 1.1e-16 rad).

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22 (AB27 holds 12.6% of its leg-frames, the most; AB06 0.9%, the least)
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes; 3,123 of 3,146 recordings
  • Share of the data. 35,959 of 84,793 observations (42.4%): 32,836 of 81,647 strides (40.2%) and 3,123 of 3,146 whole recordings. 4.2% of phase rows and 7.0% of time rows have a missing angle

Example. The figure gives the held share for all 22 participants; nothing was drawn. AB27's right thigh marker leaves the rigid thigh in about 22% of right-leg frames (3,570 observations in 142 recordings).

What else this could affect

  • Left out Angles, angle speeds, power and pressure point in held frames. Empty with the share of held frames in the observation notes. How we know: 520,406 of 12,247,050 phase rows and 1,012,525 of 14,415,683 time rows have a missing angle.
  • Checked, not affected Ground forces and moments. They do not use the marker fit and are kept in held frames.
  • Open limit Sudden-jump check across a held gap. A jump across a missing stretch is not tested.
  • Open limit AB27 right leg. About 22% of right-leg frames are held because of the thigh marker; right-leg angles of AB27 have gaps. How we know: 3,570 observations in 142 recordings.

Observations that fail a coarse limit

Sudden jumps of 30 degrees or more between neighbouring samples

Kept and flagged, nothing changed

What was wrong. A joint or segment angle changes by 30 degrees or more between two neighbouring samples, 5 ms apart. At that rate the angle would move much faster than a person can.

How we know. The check runs on the 200 Hz time table. A stride fails when a jump falls inside its span. Testing the 150-sample phase grid directly was rejected: Georgia Tech 2023 showed that this makes false jumps by resampling. A second agent recomputed the check for 292 recordings and 50 strides with 0 mismatches.

What we did. Observations with a jump stay in the full version with the failure recorded and are left out of the cleaned version. Nothing is smoothed, unwrapped or corrected. No angle was found beyond 180 degrees anywhere, so no whole-turn wrap is present.

How it was checked. All 84,793 assessment lines were recounted against the summary: 0 mismatches. Failed observations are rows 'failed a coarse limit' in the left-out list.

How much was affected

  • Participants. Spread across participants; the most failing observations (counting every limit) are AB21 96, AB25 88, AB11 84, AB17 77, AB12 73 and AB10 73
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes; 743 of 3,146 whole recordings (23.6%) contain a failing angle
  • Share of the data. 1,134 of 84,793 observations (1.3%) fail the jump check; 1,246 fail at least one check

Example. The figure counts jump samples by signal in the time table; nothing was drawn.

What else this could affect

  • Left out 1,134 observations in the cleaned version. They stay in the full version with the failure noted. How we know: Combined with the other limits, 1,246 observations are cleaned away.
  • Open limit Cause of the jumps. Whether they are marker swaps, fit failures or something else was not examined.
  • Open limit Angle speeds and power at a jump. They are computed from the same angles and are not limit-checked, so a jump shows up in them.
  • Open limit The 30 degree threshold. A coarse limit written before the data were read. It is a draft, not a reference range.
  • Not checked Jumps across held gaps. Not tested.

Ankle angles outside the joint limit (zero point unreviewed)

Kept and flagged, nothing changed

What was wrong. The joint-range check allows hip −50 to 150°, knee −30 to 165° and ankle −80 to 50°. 186 observations fall outside, nearly all at the ankle. The ten largest ankle values are 167 to 178°, which is 117 to 128° beyond the upper limit.

How we know. The limits are the same numbers used for Umich 2021 and Georgia Tech 2023, written before any of these data were read. A second agent recomputed them for 2 participants with 0 mismatches.

What we did. Nothing was changed. Failing observations stay in the full version and are left out of the cleaned version. Caveat: the ankle angle is the difference of the foot and shank angles measured against gravity, and where it is zero has not been reviewed. The ankle failures may reflect that zero point and are not evidence of measurement errors.

How it was checked. Q01 passes with a note: the 186 ankle failures should not be read as measurement errors until the zero point is reviewed.

How much was affected

  • Participants. Spread across participants (for example AB17 44 failing observations, AB10 18, AB30 16)
  • Kinds of recordings. Ramp recordings hold the ten largest values (AB30, AB23, AB24); other modes were not broken down
  • Share of the data. 186 of 84,793 observations (0.2%); 915 ankle samples (565 in the time table, 350 in the phase table)

Example. The second figure shows the ten largest failing values, chosen by size (not at random): all ankle angles in ramp recordings of AB30, AB23 and AB24.

What else this could affect

  • Open limit Ankle zero point. Where the ankle angle is zero has not been reviewed, so a passing ankle angle does not prove the zero is right.
  • Open limit Ankle speed and power. They are computed from the same angle and inherit its zero point where the angle is off.
  • Left out Failing observations in the cleaned version. 186 joint-range failures, 93 of which also fail the jump check.
  • Not checked Cause of the largest values. Not examined.

Which strides are in the package

Steps that cross label windows follow the authors' rule

Left out, accounted for

What was wrong. A source step can cross the boundary between two label windows, for example from level walking into a ramp, so the windows alone do not give it one label.

How we know. The authors' own published STRIDES rule decides, as chosen for this dataset. For the authors' right-leg intervals, 106,818 matched ours with start and end within 5 ms (one sample), 3,390 were extra partial first or last intervals, and none was missed. A separate parser re-read the text of the authors' script and re-classified every step.

What we did. Steps the rule drops are left out and listed with the reason (23,393 dropped, 2,076 left-leg steps with no matching case). The rule was published for the right leg only; the same rule was applied to the left leg and flagged. The authors' class 'stand' never occurs. The treadmill trial window is dropped, and treadmill steps with more than two belt speeds are left out (3,523 of the 23,393).

How it was checked. Every step has one disposition: 107,367 steps equal 81,647 exported plus 25,720 listed.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes; left out: level ground 1,952 steps, ramps 12,960, stairs 7,034, treadmill 3,774
  • Share of the data. 25,720 of 107,367 source steps (24.0%) are left out, plus 1 whole recording; the authors' rule on the left leg touches 39,868 strides (48.8% of 81,647)

Example. The figure counts every step by mode; nothing was drawn.

What else this could affect

  • Left out 25,720 steps and 1 recording. Each is a row in the left-out list with its reason. How we know: AB27 treadmill recording 6 has too few fitted thigh markers and is left out whole (251 steps).
  • Open limit Left-leg strides. The authors published the rule for the right leg only. 2,076 left-leg steps had no matching case in it. How we know: Stairs 1,266, ramps 758, level ground 52.
  • Checked, not affected Signal values inside kept strides. Only the choice of strides depends on the rule.
  • Not checked Whether dropped steps are poor data. The rule is the authors' label logic, not a quality judgment.

How angles and power were made

Joint centres come from the authors' scaled model

Kept and flagged, nothing changed

What was wrong. Joint centres are not measured. They come from the authors' body model, scaled to each person: the hip centre is the model's hip joint in the pelvis, placed by the pelvis marker fit, and the knee and ankle centres are the model's joint positions in the shank, placed by the shank marker fit, frame by frame. The segment angles, and the hip, knee and ankle angles built from them, depend on where the model puts those centres.

How we know. Each frame's fit is checked against the markers (see held frames). The knee is placed from the shank fit, because the scaled models give the thigh no fixed knee position (the knee moves with the angle); the route through the thigh is kept only as a cross-check. The marker pelvis angle follows the authors' pelvis model to within 0.6 to 2.2° RMS after a constant offset is removed (8 recordings from an earlier review and 24 drawn at random, one per mode for the six newest participants).

What we did. Nothing was corrected. Angles are used as the model and markers give them.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes
  • Share of the data. Every observation with an angle: all 84,793; AB28's pelvis offset touches 4,137

Example. The figure shows all 32 recordings that were compared; the 24 were drawn with a recorded seed.

What else this could affect

  • Open limit Left and right ankle height. Right minus left ankle-centre height has a median of +2.3 mm (−15 to +14 mm across participants). It is participant-specific, and whether it is fit error or real posture is unresolved.
  • Open limit AB28 pelvis angle. About −24° in every mode, while others show −4 to −18°. It is consistent within the participant (probably marker placement or posture, not a sign error) and was not corrected. How we know: 4,137 observations in 142 recordings.
  • Checked, not affected Moments and ground forces. They are the authors' published values and are not recomputed from our joint centres.
  • Not checked Joint centres against an independent measurement. No independent measurement of joint centres was used.

Power is the published moment times LocoHub's joint speed

Kept and flagged, nothing changed

What was wrong. A power value must agree with its moment and its joint speed. The standard of 24 November 2026 (D-REF-5) defines hip, knee and ankle power as the published moment times LocoHub's own joint angular velocity of the same leg.

How we know. The export already followed this rule: the build compared every row of every export file, in both tables and 12 power columns, and found 0 deviating rows. A second agent reproduced it for 67,842 sampled rows and 6 joint-sides to a relative tolerance of 1e-9.

What we did. No value was changed. The package only verifies the rule. Power is missing wherever the moment or the speed is missing. LocoHub's joint speed is the central difference of the joint angle over the two neighbouring 200 Hz samples, with no smoothing.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes
  • Share of the data. All 84,793 observations; 78.1% of phase rows (hip, this leg) and 71.6% of time rows (hip, left) have a power value

Example. The figure lists all 12 columns with rows compared; nothing was drawn.

What else this could affect

  • Left out Power in held frames and masked stance. Missing where the speed or the moment is missing. How we know: See the held-frames and masked-stance cards.
  • Open limit Power at angle jumps and extremes. Power has no limit of its own, so it carries any jump or offset of the angle.
  • Not checked Joint powers against any reference. They are limit-free, so not assessed. A missing or unassessed signal is not evidence of passing.

Source and checks

The source is the authors' 2024 copy, without publisher checksums

Kept and flagged, nothing changed

What was wrong. The source files of all 22 participants come from the authors' own 2024 copy. It carries no publisher checksums, so the source bytes cannot be checked against the checksummed 2021 publisher archive. For 21 participants nothing was compared.

How we know. For AB17 a file-by-file comparison of the 2021 archive's central directory with the 2024 copy found 1,464 files identical in size and checksum, 136 only in 2024 (133 'ik_offset' files and 3 static files), and 2 ramp joint-moment files that differ. The listing shows every ramp joint-moment file rewritten on 23 and 24 September 2021, after the 2021 archive (listing times are metadata, not proof); 59 of AB17's 61 such files still match the archive's checksums.

What we did. Nothing was changed. The limitation is written in the package and in its notes.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22; compared with the 2021 archive: AB17 only
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes
  • Share of the data. All 84,793 observations

Example. The figure shows all 22 participants and which one was compared.

What else this could affect

  • Open limit Ramp joint moments. Their files are listed as rewritten after the 2021 archive. For AB17 two of them differ from it; what changed inside them was not decoded. How we know: AB17 ramp recordings 1 (right, 01_01) and 6 (right, 01_04).
  • Not checked Source bytes of 21 participants. Not compared with the 2021 release.
  • Checked, not affected File integrity of the copy. All 22 participants' files were read without failure, and the package hashes are bound to the guarded-job receipts.

The leg-plane glitch check could not be run

Not checked

What was wrong. The check asks whether a leg's side-view plane jumps away from the walking direction. The export has no per-frame plane, so the check cannot be computed from it.

How we know. Every observation is recorded as not checked for this rule. The number of held bad-fit frames is counted instead and is on the held-frames card.

What we did. Nothing was invented. A not-checked state is never a pass. Recomputing it from the markers would need a new native job and was not done.

How much was affected

  • Participants. 22 of 22
  • Kinds of recordings. All four modes
  • Share of the data. All 84,793 observations

Example. The figure shows how every check came out, for all observations.

What else this could affect

  • Not checked Plane glitches in leg angles. Some may remain in the data.
  • Open limit Other checks that did not run everywhere. The left-right side check is missing in 40,800 strides and not applicable to the 3,146 whole recordings.

What we checked and what we did not

Checked

  • Every exported observation set was read back by separate code: the observations equal the exports' own lists, the 150-sample grid runs 0 to 100 percent, time is strictly increasing, the cleaned version is the full version minus exactly the 1,246 failed observations, and all file hashes match.
  • A random sample (seed 20261207; AB09, AB10, AB28; 30 strides and 3 recordings each, 5,544 column values) equals the export value for value, and power equals moment times joint speed on every sampled row.
  • The first export was checked on all 81,647 strides and 3,146 recordings against the processed tables (largest difference 5.7e-14), and every force of 88 random recordings was re-derived from the 1 kHz plate (largest difference 1.6e-12 body weights).
  • Power equals moment times joint speed on every row of both tables for all 12 power columns: 0 deviating rows.
  • Joint angles against coarse limits (hip −50 to 150°, knee −30 to 165°, ankle −80 to 50°), angles for jumps of 30° or more between neighbouring samples, the pressure point against 0.5 m forward and 0.3 m up and sideways of the foot, and a left-right side check. 83,547 of 84,793 observations pass; 1,246 fail. A second agent recomputed the assessment with 0 mismatches.
  • The left toe-off rule reproduces the right foot's source toe-off: median 0 ms, 95th percentile 15 ms, worst 195 ms over 20,447 strides.
  • Every item left out of either version has a row and a reason in the left-out list: 26,967 rows.

Not checked

  • Joint speeds, moments, powers, ground forces and the foot-frame pressure point beyond its coarse limit have no limit, so they were not assessed. A missing or unassessed signal is not evidence of passing.
  • The leg-plane glitch check (P1) could not be run on any observation.
  • Where the ankle angle is zero has not been reviewed.
  • The limits are a draft written before any of these data were read. They are not approved reference ranges.
  • Source files of 21 of 22 participants were not compared with the 2021 publisher archive.
  • The rebuilt left toe-off was tested on the right foot only, not against an independent left event.
  • The cause of the early heel strikes, of the angle jumps and of the largest ankle values was not examined.
  • The cause of 475,592 force samples missing at the first export without a mask was not diagnosed per case.

A passing check is not scientific approval. This page approves the conversion arithmetic and the file structure only.
